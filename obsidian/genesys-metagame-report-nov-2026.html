1. Executive Summary & Genesys Macro Economics

The Genesys format operates under two inviolable structural constraints: a strict 100-point deckbuilding budget across Main, Extra, and Side deck combined, and an absolute mechanical ban on all Link and Pendulum Monsters. Utilizing the ygo-meta-mcp v1.1.2 Bayesian consensus engine, the Obsidian TCG cell identified four primary dynamics governing competitive Genesys play:

Key Finding 1: Blitzclique Dominates via 0-Point Core Maximization
Blitzclique stands as the undisputed Tier-1 titan with 19.17% consensus share (29.41% on YGOPRODeck, 12.89% on Yu-Gi-Oh! Meta). Because its entire core engine costs 0 points, Blitzclique pilots preserve 100% of their point budget for devastating board-breakers and pointed tech, generating overwhelming tempo without budget sacrifice.
Key Finding 2: The Ghost Ogre (4 Pts) vs Ash Blossom (18 Pts) Point Divide
At only 4 points each, a full playset of Ghost Ogre & Snow Rabbit costs merely 12 points (12% of total budget). In contrast, Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring costs 18 points per copy (54 points for a playset). As a result, Ghost Ogre has achieved 91.2% adoption in top cuts as the format's gold standard hand trap, while Ash Blossom has become a specialized luxury tech.
Key Finding 3: Angelechy Holds Solid Tier-2 Positioning
Angelechy captures 10.55% consensus share (15.97% on YGOPRODeck, 7.22% on Yu-Gi-Oh! Meta) as the primary control pillar. Its bastion-style board presence and modular compatibility with secondary engines (White Forest, Blitzclique) make it the most versatile control strategy in the zero-Link landscape.
Key Finding 4: Point Efficiency in Tech Staples (Typhoon 0 Pts & Imperm 8 Pts)
Because point conservation is paramount, 0-point generic removal cards like Typhoon (0 pts) alongside high-impact budget staples like Infinite Impermanence (8 pts) and Nibiru (8 pts) provide immense competitive leverage, allowing pilots to build dense disruption suites while staying under the 100-point ceiling.
