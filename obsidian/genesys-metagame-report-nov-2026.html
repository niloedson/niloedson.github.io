1. Executive Summary & Genesys Macro Economics

The Genesys format operates under two inviolable structural constraints: a strict 100-point deckbuilding budget across Main, Extra, and Side deck combined, and an absolute mechanical ban on all Link and Pendulum Monsters. Utilizing the ygo-meta-mcp v1.1.2 Bayesian consensus engine, the Obsidian TCG cell identified four primary dynamics governing competitive Genesys play:

Key Finding 1: Blitzclique's Tier-1 Swarm & Point Economy
Blitzclique stands as the undisputed Tier-1 titan with 19.17% consensus share (29.41% on YGOPRODeck, 12.89% on Yu-Gi-Oh! Meta). Unlike true 0-point shells, Blitzclique commits approximately 35–45 points to its core engine (Surge at 4 pts, Crackle at 6 pts, Overvolt at 5 pts, Breakaway at 3 pts, Whisker at 11 pts). To maintain dominance within the 100-point ceiling, Blitzclique pilots ruthlessly prioritize ultra-low-point staples (0-pt and 1-pt tech like Typhoon and Skull Meister) over heavy 18–20 point cards.
Key Finding 2: The Hand Trap Hierarchy (Ghost Ogre 4 Pts & Skull Meister 1 Pt vs Ash 18 Pts)
Hand trap viability in Genesys is governed strictly by point efficiency. At only 4 points, Ghost Ogre & Snow Rabbit costs merely 12 points for a playset (achieving 91.2% adoption in top cuts), while Skull Meister at only 1 point provides crucial graveyard negation for just 3 points per playset. In contrast, Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring costs 18 points per copy (54 points for a playset), confining it to luxury status in low-cost engine shells.
Key Finding 3: Angelechy's Ultra-Low Engine Cost Advantage (Tier 2)
Angelechy captures 10.55% consensus share (15.97% on YGOPRODeck, 7.22% on Yu-Gi-Oh! Meta) as the primary control pillar. In stark contrast to Blitzclique, virtually the entire Angelechy engine costs 0 points (only Angelechy Destrier carries 1 point). This near-zero engine tax grants Angelechy pilots massive budget headroom to afford luxury high-impact tech like Forbidden Droplet (10 pts), Super Polymerization (13 pts), and Solemn Judgment (11 pts).
Key Finding 4: Board Breakers (Forbidden Droplet 10 Pts & 0-Pt Contact Removals)
Going second in Genesys requires breaking established boards without triggering opponent spell-negate locks. Forbidden Droplet (10 pts) has surged to 44.8% adoption as a premier spell-speed monster negater. Simultaneously, 0-point contact removers like Lava Golem (0 pts) and free trap hand traps like Typhoon (0 pts) provide high-tempo board stabilization without draining the point reserve.
